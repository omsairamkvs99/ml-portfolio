Opening the black box

Large language models like GPT, Claude, Gemini, and Llama now help with medical notes, loan reviews, and legal research. Yet even their builders cannot fully explain why a model gives a particular answer. This page explains what explainable AI is, why it is so hard, how validation and metrics fill the gap, and what the major AI labs are doing to see inside.

LAYER 1The surface

What is explainable AI, and why does it matter?

An AI system can be accurate and still be impossible to trust if no one can say why it made a decision. Explainability is what turns a confident answer into one that people can check, question, and correct.

Explainable AI (XAI)

The set of methods that make an AI system's outputs understandable to the people affected by them. NIST's principles say a good explanation should actually be given, be meaningful to its audience, accurately reflect how the system reached its result, and admit the limits of what the system knows.

Phillips et al., 2021 (NIST IR 8312)

Regulators now expect it

  • EU AI ActSince August 2, 2025, providers of general purpose AI models must keep technical documentation and publish a summary of their training content.
  • NIST AI Risk Management FrameworkLists "explainable and interpretable" and "accountable and transparent" among the characteristics of trustworthy AI.
European Commission, n.d.; NIST, 2023

Healthcare

A doctor needs to know why a model flagged a scan or summarized a chart a certain way before acting on it. An unexplained error here can hurt a patient.

Finance

Lenders must be able to tell an applicant why they were denied credit. "The model said so" is not an acceptable reason, legally or ethically.

Law

Lawyers have filed briefs with case citations that an AI invented. Transparency and verification are what keep a fluent answer from becoming a false record.

LAYER 2The obstacles

Why LLMs are so hard to explain

The difficulty is not just size. The way these models store knowledge, the way they explain themselves, and the way companies release them all make transparency harder.

Spotlight

When a model explains itself, is it telling the truth?

Anthropic gave reasoning models a hidden hint toward an answer, then checked whether the model's step by step reasoning admitted using it. Most of the time, it did not. In a separate study, Claude described the school method for adding 36 + 59 while its internal circuits were actually doing something different.

LAYER 3Measuring trust

Validation and metrics: trust you can measure

Since no one can read a model's mind directly, we judge it by how it behaves on data it has never seen. Validation is the process; metrics are the scorecard.

01Hold out dataKeep test data separate so the model is graded on unseen examples
02Cross validateRotate which data is held out so one lucky split does not mislead
03BenchmarkScore against shared test sets so models can be compared fairly
04Human reviewPeople rate quality, safety, and helpfulness that numbers miss
05Red teamExperts try to make the model fail, leak data, or cause harm
06MonitorKeep measuring after launch, because real use drifts from tests

Beyond accuracy: Stanford's HELM scorecard

The Holistic Evaluation of Language Models tests each model on seven measures across many scenarios.

AccuracyDoes it get the right answer?
CalibrationDoes its confidence match how often it is right?
RobustnessDoes it hold up when inputs are reworded or have typos?
FairnessDoes performance stay steady across groups?
BiasDoes it lean on stereotypes?
ToxicityDoes it produce harmful language?
EfficiencyHow much compute and time does it use?
Liang et al., 2022

Metrics can be fooled by leaked tests

OpenAI found parts of several benchmarks, including BIG-bench and GSM-8K, inside GPT-4's training data. A model that has already seen the test will score better than it really is.

OpenAI, 2023

Polish can hurt honesty

GPT-4's pretrained model was well calibrated, but post training made it noticeably more overconfident. Calibration is the metric that tells users when to double check.

OpenAI, 2023
LAYER 4Looking inside

How the labs are opening the box

Explainability techniques range from studying a model from the outside to tracing individual circuits inside it. The deeper the method, the more faithful the explanation, and the more expensive it is to produce.

COREWhat it adds up to

Trust is built in layers

Explanations are not proof.

Ask a model why it answered and you get a story. Check that story against evidence before relying on it.

Measure more than accuracy.

Calibration, robustness, fairness, and bias reveal problems a single accuracy score hides.

Inside research is real but early.

Labs can now find millions of concepts inside models, yet still explain only a fraction of what they compute.

Keep people in the loop.

In healthcare, finance, and law, a human who can question the output is still the best safeguard.
